The leadership challenge

A global workforce is not a map of labor locations. It is a system for creating capability, coordinating work, and sustaining trust across boundaries.

Our perspective

Workforce strategy begins with the work—not the labor market.

Many global workforce decisions begin with cost, availability, or a preferred location. Those factors matter, but they are insufficient.

Leaders must first understand the work: what requires proximity, what can be distributed, where judgment sits, how knowledge moves, which capabilities are strategic, and how accountability will operate across distance.

A sound global workforce strategy balances economics with capability, resilience, coordination, culture, and the organization’s long-term ability to learn.

Questions we help leaders answer

Design the workforce as an operating system.

01

What work should be located where?

Match work characteristics, customer needs, capability, risk, and collaboration requirements to location choices.

02

Which capabilities are strategic?

Distinguish what the organization must own from what can be accessed through partners or markets.

03

How should distributed work be coordinated?

Design interfaces, decision rights, routines, technology, and leadership for distance.

04

What risks are we concentrating?

Consider geopolitical, talent, vendor, regulatory, operational, and knowledge dependencies.

05

How will culture and trust travel?

Build shared expectations and relationships rather than assuming values transfer through communication.

06

How will the model adapt over time?

Treat location and sourcing choices as a portfolio that must evolve with strategy and capability.

Capability planning
